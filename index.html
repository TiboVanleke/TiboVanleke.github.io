Reading / recommendations welcome

What stays open after the last page?

Reading is where I let questions remain unresolved for longer than a paper deadline usually allows. I am drawn to books that offer a framework rather than a verdict—and to people with a recommendation they are willing to defend.

A useful book does not have to win the argument. It has to leave a better one behind.

The shelf below is deliberately short: books I can actually say something about, not a decorative list of everything I have finished.

Recommend a book

Six books / no ranking

A shelf I would happily argue about.

Not necessarily the six “best” books I have read. These are the ones that gave me a durable question, a useful method, or a line of thought I still return to.

01

Gödel, Escher, Bach

Douglas Hofstadter

Formal systems, strange loops, minds, and the rare kind of ambition that changes what a book can be.

02

Antifragile

Nassim Nicholas Taleb

The one Taleb entry: a vocabulary for uncertainty, fragility, and systems that improve through disorder.

03

How to Solve It

George Pólya

A practical bridge between mathematical problem-solving and the craft of helping someone else learn it.

04

The Stranger

Albert Camus

Spare, unsettling, and much harder to forget than its length suggests.

05

Bullshit Jobs

David Graeber

A provocative lens on work, meaning, and the strange institutions people learn to treat as inevitable.

06

Parkinson’s Law

C. Northcote Parkinson

Short, mischievous, and uncomfortably observant about organizations.

Short reads that overperform

The Basic Laws of Human Stupidity — Carlo M. Cipolla

The Usefulness of Useless Knowledge — Abraham Flexner

Two compact arguments with far more afterlife than their page counts suggest.
